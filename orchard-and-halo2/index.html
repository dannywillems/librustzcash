05 - Orchard and Halo 2

Dedicated companion courses

This chapter places Orchard inside the librustzcash workspace: how the crate is wired in, what an Action is at protocol level, where Halo 2 sits in the stack. The technical detail belongs in two dedicated companion courses:

  • dannywillems.github.io/orchard for the Orchard circuit, gadgets, key tree, and Sinsemilla, built from zcash/orchard.
  • dannywillems.github.io/halo2 for the proving system itself (PLONKish, IPA, transcript, custom gates, lookups), built from zcash/halo2.

Read this chapter first to place Orchard in the workspace; jump to the companion courses for the cryptographic mechanics.

Goal​

Sapling solved the privacy story but inherits Groth16 and a trusted setup. Orchard is its successor: a cleaner abstraction (unified Action instead of separate Spend/Output), a new proof system (Halo 2), and curves chosen for recursion (Pallas/Vesta cycle).

This chapter is the narrative introduction. For the full Orchard key catalog (including the distinction that nk\mathsf{nk}nk is a field element, not a curve point, and the role of rivk\mathsf{rivk}rivk), see chapter 23. For the Action circuit's clauses written out one by one (Sinsemilla path, the ρnew=nfold\rho^{\text{new}} = \mathsf{nf}^{\text{old}}ρnew=nfold trick, flag-conditional logic), see chapter 24.

This chapter develops the math behind Orchard and the relevant Halo 2 internals. The implementation lives in the orchard crate (separate repo); we describe what is essential for understanding the integration in zcash_primitives, zcash_keys, pczt, and zcash_client_backend.

1. Pallas and Vesta​

Pallas and Vesta are two short Weierstrass curves over prime fields Fp,Fq\mathbb{F}_p, \mathbb{F}_qFp​,Fq​ with p,qp, qp,q both 255-bit primes satisfying

#EPallas(Fp)  =  q,#EVesta(Fq)  =  p.\#E_{\text{Pallas}}(\mathbb{F}_p) \;=\; q, \qquad \#E_{\text{Vesta}}(\mathbb{F}_q) \;=\; p.#EPallas​(Fp​)=q,#EVesta​(Fq​)=p.

That is, the base field of each curve equals the scalar field of the other. Pictorially:

Fp  ⟶Pallas  Fq  ⟶Vesta  Fp.\mathbb{F}_p \;\underset{\text{Pallas}}{\longrightarrow}\; \mathbb{F}_q \;\underset{\text{Vesta}}{\longrightarrow}\; \mathbb{F}_p.Fp​Pallas⟶​Fq​Vesta⟶​Fp​.

This 2-cycle (p,qp, qp,q are sometimes called "amicable primes") allows encoding a verifier of one curve inside the circuit over the other, which is what powers recursive SNARKs (proofs of proofs).

The curves have prime order, complete addition formulas, no cofactor quirks. Equations:

  • Pallas: y2=x3+5y^2 = x^3 + 5y2=x3+5 over Fp\mathbb{F}_pFp​.
  • Vesta: y2=x3+5y^2 = x^3 + 5y2=x3+5 over Fq\mathbb{F}_qFq​.

The prime moduli are

p=0x40000000000000000000000000000000 224698fc094cf91b 992d30ed00000001,p = 0\text{x}40000000000000000000000000000000\,224698fc094cf91b\,992d30ed00000001,p=0x40000000000000000000000000000000224698fc094cf91b992d30ed00000001, q=0x40000000000000000000000000000000 224698fc0994a8dd 8c46eb2100000001.q = 0\text{x}40000000000000000000000000000000\,224698fc0994a8dd\,8c46eb2100000001.q=0x40000000000000000000000000000000224698fc0994a8dd8c46eb2100000001.

Orchard arithmetises its circuit over Pallas (so the proof is over Vesta's base field, but the circuit itself manipulates Pallas scalars Fq\mathbb{F}_qFq​ which equal Vesta base field). This circular dependence is the whole point of the cycle.

In code: the pasta_curves crate, with types pallas::Base, pallas::Scalar, pallas::Point, pallas::Affine; the corresponding Vesta aliases live in src/vesta.rs. The underlying field and curve implementations are in src/fields/ and src/curves.rs.

2. Sinsemilla​

Orchard's algebraic hash is Sinsemilla, a Pedersen-hash variant tuned to be cheap inside Halo 2 with lookup tables.

Construction​

Fix a "fragment size" K=10K = 10K=10 bits and two functions:

  • S ⁣:{0,1}K→EPallas(Fp)S \colon \{0,1\}^K \to E_{\text{Pallas}}(\mathbb{F}_p)S:{0,1}K→EPallas​(Fp​), a domain-separated map from 10-bit strings to Pallas points.
  • Q ⁣:D→EPallas(Fp)Q \colon \mathcal{D} \to E_{\text{Pallas}}(\mathbb{F}_p)Q:D→EPallas​(Fp​), a domain-separated map from a domain string DDD to a Pallas point.

Then for an input bit string m=m0m1…mKn−1m = m_0 m_1 \ldots m_{Kn-1}m=m0​m1​…mKn−1​ split into nnn chunks of KKK bits m(0),…,m(n−1)m^{(0)}, \ldots, m^{(n-1)}m(0),…,m(n−1):

SinsemillaD(m)  =  An−1,where\mathsf{Sinsemilla}_D(m) \;=\; A_{n-1}, \quad \text{where}SinsemillaD​(m)=An−1​,where A0  =  Q(D),Ai+1  =  Incomplete(Incomplete(Ai,S(m(i))),Ai).A_0 \;=\; Q(D), \qquad A_{i+1} \;=\; \mathsf{Incomplete}\bigl( \mathsf{Incomplete}(A_i, S(m^{(i)})), A_i \bigr).A0​=Q(D),Ai+1​=Incomplete(Incomplete(Ai​,S(m(i))),Ai​).

Here Incomplete(P1,P2)\mathsf{Incomplete}(P_1, P_2)Incomplete(P1​,P2​) is incomplete addition (works as long as P1≠±P2P_1 \neq \pm P_2P1​=±P2​, which is enforced by the protocol's choice of points). The doubled-add pattern is a cheap way to use lookup tables for SSS inside Halo 2.

Sinsemilla is parameterised by a personalisation, e.g. "z.cash:Orchard-NoteCommit-r", "z.cash:Orchard-MerkleCRH".

Note commitment​

The Orchard note commitment:

NoteCommit(rcm,gd,pkd,v,ρ,ψ)=SinsemillaDnc ⁣(repr(gd) ∥ repr(pkd) ∥ repr(v) ∥ repr(ρ) ∥ repr(ψ))+[rcm]Rnc.\mathsf{NoteCommit}(\mathsf{rcm}, g_d, \mathsf{pk}_d, v, \rho, \psi) = \mathsf{Sinsemilla}_{D_{\text{nc}}}\!\bigl( \text{repr}(g_d) \,\|\, \text{repr}(\mathsf{pk}_d) \,\|\, \text{repr}(v) \,\|\, \text{repr}(\rho) \,\|\, \text{repr}(\psi) \bigr) + [\mathsf{rcm}] R_{\text{nc}}.NoteCommit(rcm,gd​,pkd​,v,ρ,ψ)=SinsemillaDnc​​(repr(gd​)∥repr(pkd​)∥repr(v)∥repr(ρ)∥repr(ψ))+[rcm]Rnc​.

Notice: unlike Sapling, ρ\rhoρ and ψ\psiψ are part of the commitment. This bakes the nullifier randomness directly into the note rather than deriving it from the position.

Merkle hash​

The Orchard Merkle tree hash:

MerkleCRHℓ(x,y)=ExtractPallas ⁣(SinsemillaDMH ⁣(layerℓ ∥ x ∥ y)),\mathsf{MerkleCRH}_\ell(x, y) = \mathsf{ExtractPallas}\!\Bigl( \mathsf{Sinsemilla}_{D_{\text{MH}}}\!\bigl( \text{layer}_\ell \,\|\, x \,\|\, y \bigr) \Bigr),MerkleCRHℓ​(x,y)=ExtractPallas(SinsemillaDMH​​(layerℓ​∥x∥y)),

with DMH="z.cash:Orchard-MerkleCRH"D_{\text{MH}} = \text{"z.cash:Orchard-MerkleCRH"}DMH​="z.cash:Orchard-MerkleCRH", layer index ℓ\ellℓ explicitly hashed in, and ExtractPallas\mathsf{ExtractPallas}ExtractPallas taking the xxx-coordinate.

3. Key tree​

Orchard's key tree is structurally similar to Sapling but uses Poseidon-based PRFs and the Pallas curve.

From a 32-byte spending key sk\mathsf{sk}sk:

ask=ToScalar(PRFskexpand(6)),\mathsf{ask} = \mathsf{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRF}^{\text{expand}}_{\mathsf{sk}}(6)\bigr),ask=ToScalar(PRFskexpand​(6)), nk=PoseidonHash(PRFskexpand(7)),\mathsf{nk} = \mathsf{PoseidonHash}(\mathsf{PRF}^{\text{expand}}_{\mathsf{sk}}(7)),nk=PoseidonHash(PRFskexpand​(7)), rivk=ToScalar(PRFskexpand(8)).\mathsf{rivk} = \mathsf{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRF}^{\text{expand}}_{\mathsf{sk}}(8)\bigr).rivk=ToScalar(PRFskexpand​(8)).

The spend-validating key is ak=SpendAuthSig.DerivePublic(ask)=[ask]GOrchardak\mathsf{ak} = \mathsf{SpendAuthSig.DerivePublic}(\mathsf{ask}) = [\mathsf{ask}] G^{\mathsf{ak}}_{\text{Orchard}}ak=SpendAuthSig.DerivePublic(ask)=[ask]GOrchardak​.

The diversifier key dk\mathsf{dk}dk and the actual diversified addresses are computed as in Sapling but using Pallas group operations:

ivk=CommitIvkrivk(ak,nk),gd=DiversifyHash(d),pkd=[ivk]gd.\mathsf{ivk} = \mathsf{CommitIvk}^{\mathsf{rivk}}(\mathsf{ak}, \mathsf{nk}), \qquad g_d = \mathsf{DiversifyHash}(d), \qquad \mathsf{pk}_d = [\mathsf{ivk}] g_d.ivk=CommitIvkrivk(ak,nk),gd​=DiversifyHash(d),pkd​=[ivk]gd​.

The full viewing key is fvk=(ak,nk,rivk,ovk)\mathsf{fvk} = (\mathsf{ak}, \mathsf{nk}, \mathsf{rivk}, \mathsf{ovk})fvk=(ak,nk,rivk,ovk).

The nullifier:

nf  =  ExtractPallas ⁣([ Hash(nk,ρ)+ψ ]Gnf  +  cm),\mathsf{nf} \;=\; \mathsf{ExtractPallas}\!\Bigl( \bigl[\, \mathsf{Hash}(\mathsf{nk}, \rho) + \psi \,\bigr] G_{\text{nf}} \;+\; \mathsf{cm} \Bigr),nf=ExtractPallas([Hash(nk,ρ)+ψ]Gnf​+cm),

where Hash\mathsf{Hash}Hash is a specific Poseidon-based PRF, ρ,ψ\rho, \psiρ,ψ are note fields, and GnfG_{\text{nf}}Gnf​ is a fixed generator. The cm\mathsf{cm}cm addition ties the nullifier to the precise commitment.

4. The Action description​

An Orchard Action combines one spend and one output into a single description:

AD  =  (cv,nf,rk,cmx,epk,Cenc,Cout),\mathsf{AD} \;=\; \bigl(\mathsf{cv}, \mathsf{nf}, \mathsf{rk}, \mathsf{cm}_x, \mathsf{epk}, C^{\text{enc}}, C^{\text{out}}\bigr),AD=(cv,nf,rk,cmx​,epk,Cenc,Cout),

shared by all Actions in a bundle:

Bundle=({ADi}i=1n,anchor,vbal,flags,π,σbind,{σspendAuth,i}).\mathsf{Bundle} = \bigl(\{\mathsf{AD}_i\}_{i=1}^n, \mathsf{anchor}, v_{\text{bal}}, \mathsf{flags}, \pi, \sigma_{\text{bind}}, \{\sigma_{\text{spendAuth},i}\}\bigr).Bundle=({ADi​}i=1n​,anchor,vbal​,flags,π,σbind​,{σspendAuth,i​}).

The flags tell whether spends and outputs are enabled in the bundle (otherwise the relevant pieces are dummies). The proof π\piπ is a single Halo 2 proof for the whole bundle, not one per Action: the Action circuit is instantiated nnn times inside one Halo 2 statement.

The Action statement (sketch)​

The prover knows, for each Action:

  • Old note: (vold,gdold,pkdold,ρold,ψold,rcmold,auth-path,ak,nk,rivk,α)(v_{\text{old}}, g_d^{\text{old}}, \mathsf{pk}_d^{\text{old}}, \rho^{\text{old}}, \psi^{\text{old}}, \mathsf{rcm}^{\text{old}}, \text{auth-path}, \mathsf{ak}, \mathsf{nk}, \mathsf{rivk}, \alpha)(vold​,gdold​,pkdold​,ρold,ψold,rcmold,auth-path,ak,nk,rivk,α).
  • New note: (vnew,gdnew,pkdnew,ψnew,rcmnew)(v_{\text{new}}, g_d^{\text{new}}, \mathsf{pk}_d^{\text{new}}, \psi^{\text{new}}, \mathsf{rcm}^{\text{new}})(vnew​,gdnew​,pkdnew​,ψnew,rcmnew).

The circuit enforces:

  1. The old note commitment is in the tree at the public anchor (or the spend flag is off so the Merkle check is skipped).
  2. The nullifier formula evaluates to the public nf\mathsf{nf}nf.
  3. The spend-auth public key relation: rk=ak+[α]Gak\mathsf{rk} = \mathsf{ak} + [\alpha] G^{\mathsf{ak}}rk=ak+[α]Gak.
  4. The new note commitment cmnew\mathsf{cm}^{\text{new}}cmnew matches the public cmx\mathsf{cm}_xcmx​ (its xxx-coordinate).
  5. Value commitment: cv=[vold]V+[rcv]R\mathsf{cv} = [v_{\text{old}}] V + [\mathsf{rcv}] Rcv=[vold​]V+[rcv]R, but in Orchard the convention is net: cv\mathsf{cv}cv commits to vold−vnewv_{\text{old}} - v_{\text{new}}vold​−vnew​, so the bundle's binding key only needs the action commitments and vbalv_{\text{bal}}vbal​.
  6. vold,vnew∈[0,264)v_{\text{old}}, v_{\text{new}} \in [0, 2^{64})vold​,vnew​∈[0,264) when enabled.
  7. ρnew=nfold\rho^{\text{new}} = \mathsf{nf}^{\text{old}}ρnew=nfold: the next note's uniqueness comes from this note's nullifier. (This is a clever trick: it removes the need to derive ρ\rhoρ from position, which would require committing to position; instead the chain of nullifiers acts as ρ\rhoρ.)
  8. ECDH and encryption consistency: epk=[esk]gdnew\mathsf{epk} = [\mathsf{esk}] g_d^{\text{new}}epk=[esk]gdnew​ matches the encrypted ciphertext.

This is a single, large circuit (the Action circuit) instantiated nnn times. Halo 2's lookup-and-permutation tooling makes a 10-Action bundle prove and verify in roughly the same wallclock time as it would in Sapling for an equivalent payload, but with no trusted setup.

Binding signature​

Identical mechanism to Sapling, just over Pallas:

bvk=∑icvi−[vbal]V,\mathsf{bvk} = \sum_i \mathsf{cv}_i - [v_{\text{bal}}] V,bvk=i∑​cvi​−[vbal​]V,

and the binding signature is RedPallas over the sighash with this implicit key. The signed bvk is computed in orchard::bundle::Bundle::binding_validating_key.

5. Halo 2 in brief​

Sapling's Groth16 has constant-size proof but needs a per-circuit trusted setup. Halo, then Halo 2, replaced the trusted setup with a nothing-up-my-sleeve universal structured reference string, at the cost of larger proofs (kilobytes instead of ∼200\sim 200∼200 bytes) and more expensive verification.

Polynomial IOPs and PLONK arithmetisation​

Halo 2 is built on a PLONK-style arithmetisation:

  • The circuit is a 2D table of cells.
  • Each row is constrained by a set of polynomial identities ("custom gates").
  • Cross-cell equality is enforced by copy constraints, implemented via a permutation argument.
  • Range checks and other "complicated" predicates use lookup arguments against precomputed tables.

Each column WjW_jWj​ of the table is interpolated as a polynomial wj(X)w_j(X)wj​(X) over a domain D⊆FD \subseteq \mathbb{F}D⊆F. The custom gates are expressed as polynomial identities

Pk(w1(X),w2(X),…,ω⋅X,…)  =  0for all X∈D,P_k(w_1(X), w_2(X), \ldots, \omega \cdot X, \ldots) \;=\; 0 \quad \text{for all } X \in D,Pk​(w1​(X),w2​(X),…,ω⋅X,…)=0for all X∈D,

where shifting by a primitive root of unity ω\omegaω encodes "next row". Verification collapses to checking a small number of polynomial evaluations of committed polynomials.

Inner Product Argument (IPA)​

The polynomial commitment in Halo 2 is IPA over Pallas. Given a polynomial f(X)=∑i=0n−1aiXif(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i X^if(X)=∑i=0n−1​ai​Xi of degree <n< n<n, a commitment is

Comm(f)  =  ∑i=0n−1[ai]Gi  ∈  EPallas(Fp),\mathsf{Comm}(f) \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} [a_i] G_i \;\in\; E_{\text{Pallas}}(\mathbb{F}_p),Comm(f)=i=0∑n−1​[ai​]Gi​∈EPallas​(Fp​),

with fixed deterministic bases {Gi}\{G_i\}{Gi​}. Opening at a point zzz uses a logarithmic-size argument that bisects the vector and folds it, producing log⁡2n\log_2 nlog2​n rounds.

The verifier is dominated by an MSM (multi-scalar multiplication) of size nnn on Pallas. This is what enables recursion: an instance of the verifier can be encoded as a circuit (since MSMs are arithmetic), and the cycle of curves makes that practical.

Orchard does not itself use recursion in production (no proof amortisation across blocks); but Halo 2 was chosen so the option is available. In practice each Orchard bundle produces one Halo 2 proof that is verified directly.

Concrete cost​

Approximate numbers for an Orchard bundle with 1 action on a modern CPU:

  • Proof size: ∼5\sim 5∼5 kB.
  • Prover time: ∼1\sim 1∼1 s (∼0.5\sim 0.5∼0.5 s per added action thereafter).
  • Verifier time: ∼10\sim 10∼10 ms.

Compared to Sapling:

  • Sapling Spend proof: ∼200\sim 200∼200 B, ∼2\sim 2∼2 s prover, ∼7\sim 7∼7 ms verifier.

The proof-size cost is the price paid for removing the trusted setup.

What you should know after this chapter​

  • That Pallas/Vesta is a 2-cycle of curves.
  • The structure of the Action circuit: unified spend+output, with flags to handle spend-only or output-only.
  • The role of Sinsemilla as the in-circuit hash.
  • Why Halo 2 was chosen: no trusted setup, recursion-ready, PLONK-style arithmetisation with lookups.
  • That Orchard's nullifier construction is fundamentally different from Sapling's: position is replaced by a chain of nullifiers.
